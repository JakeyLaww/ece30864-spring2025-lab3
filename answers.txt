Story 1 - Agile allows teams to respond quickly to changing requirements, making it ideal for dynamic projects where needs evolve over time.
Agile promotes iterative development with frequent releases, enabling faster time-to-market and continuous feedback.
Due to its iterative nature, Agile often prioritizes working software over comprehensive documentation, which can create challenges for future maintenance.
Continuous changes and evolving requirements can lead to uncontrolled scope expansion, making it harder to manage timelines and resources.

Story 2 - The Scrum Master is responsible for facilitating the Scrum process, ensuring that the team follows Agile principles, 
and removing obstacles that hinder progress, while the Product Owner makes decisions about the product vision, communicates with customers, 
and continuously refines the backlog based on feedback and business needs.

Story 3 - The three main types of meetings during a sprint cycle are sprint planning, daily stand-ups, and sprint retrospectives. 
In sprint planning, the team discusses the sprint goal and selects backlog items to work on, ensuring tasks are clearly defined.
Daily stand-ups focus on progress updates, where team members share what they worked on, what they plan to do next, and any blockers they face. 
Sprint retrospectives occur at the end of the sprint, where the team reflects on what went well and identifies areas for improvement to enhance future sprints.

Story 4 - An epic, story, bug, and issue each serve different purposes in Agile development. An epic is a large body of work that is broken down into smaller user stories and spans multiple sprints, typically representing a major feature or initiative. 
A story is a specific user requirement that delivers a piece of functionality, usually written from the user’s perspective. A bug refers to an error or defect in the software that needs to be fixed to ensure proper functionality. 
An issue is a broader term that can refer to any impediment, task, or problem that affects the development process, including technical debt, process inefficiencies, or infrastructure concerns.
